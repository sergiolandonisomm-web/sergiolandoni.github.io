A different way to discover Patagonia

Wine is one way into a much larger story

Patagonia does not reveal itself all at once. Its distances are real, the landscape changes constantly and each place follows its own rhythm.

Some journeys begin in a vineyard. Others begin around a table, with a bottle of wine, regional food and a conversation that continues longer than expected. There is no single way to experience Patagonia, and that is part of what makes travelling here so rewarding.

Wine belongs naturally within that experience. It brings together the geography of the region, the work of growers and winemakers, the local cuisine and an Argentine way of sharing food and time with others.

This is not a fixed itinerary or a catalogue of tours. Each encounter begins with the interests of the visitor and develops around the places, people and moments that can offer a meaningful view of Patagonia.

Around the table

From a vineyard terrace to an improvised meal beside a lake

Patagonia does not ask us to choose between simplicity and refinement. Both can belong naturally to the same journey.

A carefully prepared dinner overlooking a vineyard and a simple meal shared outdoors may leave equally lasting memories. What matters is not formality, but the relationship between the food, the wine, the landscape and the people gathered around the table.

Argentine hospitality is often expressed without ceremony. A bottle is opened, the food arrives, conversation continues and the meal gradually becomes something more than a reservation or an activity on an itinerary.

In Patagonia, some of the finest experiences are beautifully simple.

Patagonia at its own pace

Not everything happens exactly as planned

Travelling through Patagonia sometimes asks for time, flexibility and a willingness to adapt.

The distances can be long. Weather may change quickly. A road can take more time than expected and an itinerary may need to shift as the day unfolds.

These are not details to hide. They belong to the character of the region. Careful planning matters, but so does leaving room for the unexpected lunch, the conversation that runs late or the place that deserves more time than originally planned.

Patagonia is not perfect, and it has never needed to be. Much of its appeal lies in the feeling that the journey remains real, shaped by the landscape rather than arranged entirely around the visitor.

The best moments are not always the ones that were planned.

Different regions, different stories

Patagonia is not a single wine destination

Neuquén, Río Negro and Chubut belong to the same broad geography, but their vineyards, histories and ways of receiving visitors are very different.

Neuquén

A wine landscape still expanding

San Patricio del Chañar established the modern centre of Neuquén wine, but the province can no longer be understood through a single valley.

The rivers Neuquén and Limay, the central plateau, the Andean foothills and the northern valleys are adding new expressions and new questions to its viticulture.

Depending on the purpose of the visit, an encounter may begin in San Martín de los Andes, continue through the established vineyards or move towards smaller projects where the geography of Neuquén is still being explored.

Río Negro

The historical foundation of Patagonian wine

Río Negro brings the continuity of old vineyards, river-fed agriculture and knowledge developed over generations.

The Upper, Middle and Lower Valleys offer different ways of understanding the oldest established wine culture in Patagonia, from historic vineyards to selected contemporary projects.

My relationship with the province is personal as well as professional. It was there that my first vineyard experiences began, long before wine became the centre of my work.

Chubut

Mountain vineyards and southern frontiers

Trevelin has become one of Patagonia’s most recognised wine destinations, while the Andean Region around El Hoyo, Lago Puelo and El Bolsón brings together small wineries, growers and projects closely connected to the mountain landscape.

Farther east, selected producers across the Chubut River Valley add another dimension to the province’s still-developing wine identity.

Relationships built over years with producers, winemakers and regional professionals make it possible to approach these places with context, always according to the nature of each visit and the availability of those involved.

Beyond the vineyards

The landscape is not a backdrop

It influences the wines, the food, the distances and the way each day unfolds.

Patagonia can mean native forest and mountain lakes, dry plateaus, river valleys, snow-covered peaks or open country where the horizon seems to extend without interruption.

These differences are part of the experience. A wine may be tasted near the vineyard where it was grown, beside a lake, in a carefully chosen restaurant or around a fire after a day spent travelling through the region.

The setting changes, but the purpose remains the same: to understand wine as part of a place rather than as something separated from it.

No fixed formula

Every encounter begins with a different interest

Some visitors arrive looking for wine. Others are drawn by gastronomy, landscape, professional research or the desire to understand Patagonia beyond the usual routes.

Wine and regional gastronomy

A private tasting, a shared meal or a carefully selected table can provide an introduction to the wines of Patagonia without turning the experience into a formal class.

The wines are presented alongside the regions, people and foods that give them meaning.

Regional journeys

When time and distances allow, a journey can connect different wine landscapes through vineyards, wineries, restaurants and conversations with the people working there.

The route is considered individually and adapted to the season, the interests of the visitor and what can genuinely be arranged.

Professional and media visits

Journalists, importers, buyers, sommeliers, educators and institutions may need something different from a conventional visit.

Context, regional interpretation and carefully considered introductions can help them understand the projects and people shaping Patagonian wine.

Time to enjoy the place

Not every meaningful moment needs an itinerary

Sometimes the most memorable part of the day is simply finding the right place to stop.

A glass of wine overlooking a lake, a conversation after lunch or the changing light at the end of the afternoon can become as important as any scheduled visit.

Patagonia often rewards those who leave enough space for those moments. They cannot always be planned, but they can be recognised when they appear.

Your host

Understanding the place before shaping the journey

My role is not to lead a conventional tour, but to help visitors understand Patagonia and connect its wines with the places and people behind them.

I have lived in Patagonia for more than three decades. My first vineyard experiences came in Río Negro, followed by the development of Neuquén and later by projects across Chubut and the Andean Region.

During those years I have developed relationships with growers, winemakers, winery owners, chefs, hospitality professionals, researchers and regional institutions.

Those relationships are based on trust and cannot be treated as a catalogue. Every introduction is considered carefully, according to the purpose of the visit and the interest of everyone involved.

I remain the host, not the protagonist. The people who grow the grapes, make the wines, prepare the food and live in these places are the ones who give each encounter its real meaning.

Argentine hospitality

A place at the table

In Argentina, wine is rarely separated from food, conversation and the pleasure of receiving someone well.

Hospitality may take the form of an elegant restaurant with attentive service and views across the mountains, or a simple table arranged outdoors beside a lake. Patagonia allows both to belong naturally to the same journey.

An asado, a regional dish, a carefully chosen bottle or a relaxed conversation after lunch can reveal as much about the place as a formal tasting.

Meals here do not always end when the plates are cleared. Conversation often continues, another wine may be opened and the table becomes part of the memory of the journey.

By prior arrangement

Considered individually, never offered as a standard package

Patagonian Wine Experience is developed around each visitor rather than offered through a fixed programme.

Your interests

The first step is understanding what brings you to Patagonia, how much time you have and whether your interest is personal, professional, editorial or educational.

The right scale

Encounters are best suited to individuals, couples and small private groups. This allows the experience to remain flexible, personal and connected to the people involved.

Language support

Encounters can be considered for international visitors. English-language support may be coordinated when required, according to the nature of the group and the purpose of the visit.

Availability depends on the season, distances, regional conditions and the schedules of wineries, producers, chefs and other participants.
